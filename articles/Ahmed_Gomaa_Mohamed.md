**Ahmed Gomaa Mohamed** ({{langx|ar|أحمد جمعة محمد}}; also recorded in international results as **Gomma G. Ahmed** and **Mohamed Ahmed**) is an Egyptian [[para powerlifting|para powerlifter]]. He is a three-time [[Paralympic Games|Paralympic]] champion, winning gold in the men's 52&nbsp;kg class at the [[1992 Summer Paralympics]] in Barcelona and in the men's 56&nbsp;kg class at the [[1996 Summer Paralympics]] in Atlanta and the [[2000 Summer Paralympics]] in Sydney, and adding a silver medal in the 56&nbsp;kg class at the [[2004 Summer Paralympics]] in Athens.

## Career

### Barcelona 1992

Gomaa made his Paralympic debut at the [[1992 Summer Paralympics]] in [[Barcelona]], where he won the gold medal in the men's 52&nbsp;kg powerlifting event ahead of Chris O'Neill of the [[United States]] and [[Andrzej Greń]] of [[Poland]].<ref name="1992pl">{{cite web |title=Powerlifting at the 1992 Summer Paralympics |website=Wikipedia |url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerlifting_at_the_1992_Summer_Paralympics}}</ref> On 5 September 1992 he lifted 177.50&nbsp;kg in the 52&nbsp;kg bodyweight category, a mark recognised by the [[International Paralympic Committee]] as a world record; the IPC issued him a powerlifting diploma certifying the record.<ref name="ipcdiploma">International Paralympic Committee, Powerlifting Diploma awarded to Ahmed Mohamed Gomaa, world record 177.50&nbsp;kg, −52.00&nbsp;kg bodyweight category, Paralympic Games 1992, Barcelona, Spain, 5 September 1992. Signed by Andre Raes, IPC Secretary General.</ref> The Barcelona organising committee also presented him with a certificate of recognition and appreciation signed by Guillermo Cabezas, President of the [[International Coordinating Committee of World Sports Organizations for the Disabled|ICC]], and [[Pasqual Maragall]], President of COOB'92, for his contribution to the success of the IX Paralympic Games.<ref name="coob">Comitè Organitzador Olímpic Barcelona'92 (COOB'92), certificate of recognition and appreciation issued to Ahmed Ahmed for his contribution to the success of the IX Paralympic Games, Barcelona 1992.</ref>

### Atlanta 1996

Moving up to the men's 56&nbsp;kg class, Gomaa retained his Paralympic title at the [[1996 Summer Paralympics]] in [[Atlanta]].<ref name="egy1996">{{cite web |title=Egypt at the 1996 Summer Paralympics |website=Wikipedia |url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_at_the_1996_Summer_Paralympics}}</ref> On 7 September 1996 the [[Egyptian Supreme Council for Youth and Sports]], under its president Dr. Abdel Moneim Emara, issued him a certificate of thanks and appreciation for winning the gold medal at the Atlanta Games and for what it described as an honourable performance that raised Egypt's flag high in the field of sport.<ref name="scys">Supreme Council for Youth and Sports (Egypt), certificate of thanks and appreciation to the athlete Ahmed Gomaa Mohamed on winning a gold medal at the Atlanta Games, signed by Dr. Abdel Moneim Emara, dated 24 Safar 1417 AH / 7 September 1996.</ref>

### Sydney 2000

At the [[2000 Summer Paralympics]] in [[Sydney]] Gomaa won his third consecutive Paralympic gold medal, taking the men's 56&nbsp;kg title with a lift of 192.5&nbsp;kg that improved his own Paralympic and world records.<ref name="ahram">{{cite news |last=|first=|title=Eat your hearts out |work=[[Al-Ahram Weekly]] |issue=507 |year=2000 |url=http://weekly.ahram.org.eg/Archive/2000/507/sp1.htm}}</ref><ref name="egy2000">{{cite web |title=Egypt at the 2000 Summer Paralympics |website=Wikipedia |url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_at_the_2000_Summer_Paralympics}}</ref>

### Athens 2004

Gomaa competed at a fourth Paralympic Games in [[Athens]] in 2004, where he won the silver medal in the men's 56&nbsp;kg event with a lift of 180.0&nbsp;kg, finishing behind Wang Jian of [[China]], who lifted 185.0&nbsp;kg. The event was held on 22 September 2004.<ref name="2004pl">{{cite web |title=Powerlifting at the 2004 Summer Paralympics – Men's 56 kg |website=Wikipedia |url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerlifting_at_the_2004_Summer_Paralympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_56_kg}}</ref>

### World championships and other competition

Alongside his Paralympic career Gomaa competed on the world championship circuit. At the second World Handicapped Powerlifting Championships he set a world record in the 56&nbsp;kg section, edging the Korean lifter Keum-Jong Jung on bodyweight after both achieved the same total, and was awarded the prize for best technical consistency; a contemporary report by Lawrence Machado described the performance, together with that of his compatriot Metwaly Ibrahim Mathana, as an "awesome display of powerlifting".<ref name="press">Machado, Lawrence. "Egyptians shatter world records" (newspaper clipping, second World Handicapped Powerlifting Championships).</ref>

He also represented Egypt at the [[2003 All-Africa Games]] in [[Abuja]], [[Nigeria]], where he was accredited as athlete number 141 for the Egyptian [[National Olympic Committee]].<ref name="abuja">All Africa Games Abuja 2003, athlete accreditation card no. 141, Ahmed Gomaa Mohamed, Egypt – NOC.</ref>

## Legacy

Gomaa was part of the generation of Egyptian para powerlifters — alongside [[Fatma Omar]], Metwaly Mathna and others — that established Egypt as one of the dominant nations in the sport from the 1990s onwards.<ref name="egyparalympics">{{cite web |title=Egypt at the Paralympics |website=Wikipedia |url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_at_the_Paralympics}}</ref>

## Personal documents and name variants

International results databases have recorded Gomaa's name inconsistently, reflecting different transliterations and orderings of the Arabic name أحمد جمعة محمد. Forms appearing in official documents and results include "Ahmed Gomaa Mohamed", "Ahmed Mohamed Gomaa", "Gomma G. Ahmed" and "Mohamed Ahmed".
